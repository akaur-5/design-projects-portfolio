## Arsh Kaur 
I am a Student at University of North Carolina at Charlotte, completing my B.S in Mechanical Engineering with a concentration in Biomedical. My interest in engineering was initially influenced by my family's history in manufacturing and business, which introduced me to the practical and operational side of producing physical products. That early exposure led me to explore mechanical engineering and develop an interest in how engineering principles can be applied to improve manufacturing processes, equipment, and systems. My interest became more defined through my 2025 summer internship at Novo Nordisk as a Black Utilities Intern, where I gained experience in a pharmaceutical manufacturing environment and discovered that I particularly enjoy the combination of engineering, manufacturing, and hands-on problem-solving. This experience motivated me to begin my career with a focus on biomedical engineering applications, particularly pharmaceutical manufacturing, where I can contribute to systems that have a direct impact on the production of healthcare products. As I progress in my career, I also want to explore engineering across different industries rather than limiting myself to one field. I believe hands-on experience and exposure to unfamiliar problems are some of the best ways to develop as an engineer, challenge my existing knowledge, and discover new areas of interest. I am working toward becoming an engineer who can approach problems analytically while also understanding how engineering decisions affect real-world manufacturing environments. I value technical knowledge, adaptability, continuous learning, and the ability to communicate engineering decisions clearly. Outside of engineering, I enjoy traveling, cooking, and seeking new experiences, which reflect my broader interest in learning from unfamiliar environments and perspectives.

To me, defending an engineering decision means being able to explain not only what decision was made, but why it was made and why it is a reasonable choice compared with the available alternatives. A defensible engineering decision should be based on defined requirements, constraints, assumptions, and evidence rather than personal preference. This means being able to identify the criteria used to evaluate alternatives, explain the analysis behind the decision, and communicate the tradeoffs involved. At this point in my engineering education, I understand what it means to defend an engineering decision, but I do not believe I have fully mastered how to do it. I can explain the reasoning behind many of my choices, but I am still developing my ability to support those choices with sufficient quantitative evidence and clearly documented criteria. I expect this to be an important area of growth throughout the semester. By the end of the course, I want to be able to present an engineering decision clearly enough that another engineer can understand my reasoning, evaluate the evidence, and build upon my work without needing additional explanation.
